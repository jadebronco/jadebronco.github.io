I

The Jade Bronco Method

One lesson outlasts every market cycle: a strategy only counts if a real person can understand it, repeat it, and stick with it. The Jade Bronco Method is built on that test.

It is plain-spoken coaching in sustainable, repeatable strategy — the kind of approach a trader can run the same way on a quiet Tuesday as in a wild one. Risk is named first. Every position has a reason, an exit, and a size that lets you sleep. The jargon gets translated, the charts get explained in ordinary words, and nothing is taught that hasn't been worked live, in front of students, session after session.

That discipline runs both directions. The same man who coached traders also served as a compliance trustee and auditor — so the Method is taught the way a professional is expected to work: documented, rule-bound, and honest about what education can and cannot do.

  • ◆

    Plain-Spoken

    If it can't be explained simply, it isn't understood yet. Concepts are taught in clear language first — formulas second.

  • ◆

    Sustainable

    Built for the long haul: position sizing, defined risk, and routines a trader can maintain for years, not weeks.

  • ◆

    Repeatable

    Setups with rules, not hunches. The same analysis, the same checklist, applied the same way in almost every market condition.

IV

Roots — Where the Name Comes From

The Farm

Brian Keith Anderson was raised on a farm in northern Utah, milking cows by hand from the age of seven. Farm work sets the terms early: the weather doesn't negotiate, the animals don't wait, and a job done halfway is a job done twice. He carried that standard through scouting — he is an Eagle Scout — and into every desk and classroom since.

The Stone

Years later he took up the lapidary's wheel. From 2009 to 2019 he cut, polished, and sold variscite and turquoise jewelry under the name Jade Bronco — green Utah stone worked by hand, piece by piece, including Ni'ihau shell work. Rough rock in, finished stone out.

The Name

Jade for the stone — green, patient, harder than it looks. Bronco for the country that raised him. One name covers both halves of the workbench — the stone he cut and the markets he teaches — because the standard never changed: know your material, do the work by hand, and don't ship anything you wouldn't sign.
