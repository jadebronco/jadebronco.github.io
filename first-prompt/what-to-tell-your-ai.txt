WHAT TO GIVE YOUR AI — the condensed checklist
From https://jadebronco.com/first-prompt/ — free to copy and share.

An agent can only be as good as what it knows about you.
Hand these over, in this order:

1. YOUR FULL WORK RECORD
   Your resume plus everything it leaves out: exact
   dates, jobs between the jobs, side work, volunteer
   work, untitled skills. If it isn't in the record,
   the agent can't use it — and must never invent it.

2. YOUR CREDENTIALS, PRECISELY
   Degrees, licenses, certifications — which are
   active, which are inactive, which you passed once.
   "Previously held" and "currently holds" are
   different answers on a form.

3. YOUR FLOORS AND YOUR MAP
   Lowest pay you'll accept, where you'll work,
   remote rules, states you'd relocate to.

4. YOUR HARD NO'S
   Duties, industries, and deal-breakers that kill a
   posting on sight — including your real physical
   limits.

5. THE STRENGTHS YOUR RESUME UNDERSELLS
   What you're genuinely great at that no job title
   ever captured. (Example from the source: "very
   skilled on horseback" opened a whole lane of
   ranch and guide work.)

6. YOUR STANDING ANSWERS
   Authorization, eligibility, start date, references,
   salary expectation — answered once, in a file, so
   every form gets the same true answers.

7. YOUR NEVER-CLAIM LIST
   The skills and experience you do NOT have, written
   down, so nothing gets invented on your applications.

8. ACCESS, SAFELY
   Connect the email where confirmations and interview
   invites land. Passwords go in a secure vault —
   never in chat, never in a document.

9. YOUR AUTHORITY LINES
   What the agent may do without asking (search,
   apply, reply) and what must always come to you
   (interviews, signatures, legal and judgment calls).

10. YOUR RHYTHMS AND CONSTRAINTS
    When you're available, how you want to be reached,
    how many questions at a time. The system bends
    around the human.

11. YOUR CORRECTIONS, FOREVER
    Every time it gets something wrong, correct it
    once, in writing, and have it banked as a standing
    rule. Systems like this are built out of hundreds
    of small corrections, compounded.

THE PATTERN: give it facts, give it boundaries, give
it authority — then correct it like you'd train a
good employee.
